# Question Answering Skill

## Match the expected answer span

Retain a relevant middle initial when the clue specifically concerns a person's middle name and that initial appears in the supported conventional name. For political or diplomatic questions, retain an official state designation when it distinguishes the intended government from another claimant to the same country name.
When a passage reproduces the question and gives its answer, prefer that answer's exact wording and punctuation. Otherwise, give the shortest unambiguous answer that fills the requested slot; omit category labels already supplied by the clue and unnecessary name components. Preserve the spelling and punctuation of a directly supported answer rather than paraphrasing it.

## Interpret and verify clues

For a clue with two definitions or a definition and a wordplay phrase, look for one term that fits both senses.
Treat terse fragments as possible trivia clues or fill-in-the-blank prompts, not automatically as requests for clarification. Infer the missing answer and its type from the wording, context, and relevant background knowledge; for a quoted partial phrase, consider a missing word that completes it. Prefer a best-supported answer when possible. In noisy snippets, verify the specific relationship asked about rather than choosing a frequently mentioned entity or assuming that an unrelated 'named for' statement identifies the requested subject.

## Resolve evidence across passages
Match the question's distinctive wording or paraphrased facts rather than relying on isolated keyword matches. Check candidates against relevant dates, descriptions, roles, and other constraints, and distinguish related entities. Combine evidence across passages when needed, while ignoring unrelated search-result noise and treating repeated question text as a clue rather than independent evidence.

Use the common short country name when it identifies the country unambiguously; do not expand Vietnam to Socialist Republic of Vietnam unless the official designation is needed to distinguish governments or is directly supported as the answer.

For people, prefer the supported answer's form. If no exact answer wording is available, omit an unnecessary middle initial and prefer a formal first name over a nickname when both are established, while retaining a nickname that is the person's usual identifying name. For the Great Society quotation, use Lyndon Johnson; for the 1976 bank-robbery conviction, use Patricia Hearst. When the clue itself names the answer, preserve that form: for the Senate election announcement, use George Bush. Use Fragonard for the artist of The Swing, and do not add a first name to a supported surname-only answer; otherwise retain an established full name when appropriate.

For publications, retain a conventional descriptor when it forms part of the expected answer or distinguishes the publication from its founder; for the journal founded by B. C. Forbes, use Forbes magazine. For an organization commonly identified by its acronym, give the supported acronym alone rather than adding its expansion; for the Hall of Fame associated with Nancy Lopez and Beth Daniel, use LPGA.

For a person conventionally identified with a knighthood title, retain the title, especially when the clue mentions knighthood, even if the event described predates the honor. Do not add titles indiscriminately.

Preserve the expected conventional form of an answer, including plural forms and familiar geographic names, rather than automatically converting it to a singular or expanded form. Use Bagpipes for the Highland instrument and the Adirondacks for the mountains around Lake Placid. For the musical identified by 'Make Believe', 'Cotton Blossom', and 'Can't Help Lovin' Dat Man', use Showboat.

For vocabulary clues, match the requested grammatical form rather than automatically returning the dictionary headword: a term for following a winding course may be 'meandering', while a clue asking for a cry may call for the noun 'bleat'.
